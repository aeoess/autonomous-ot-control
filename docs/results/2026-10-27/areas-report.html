The heating cables dominate. As found they run on an air thermostat (−8..+3 °C) and heat a dry ramp for most of the winter; on the ground sensor they run only while the surface is wet and freezing. That share rests on the twin's assumed cable ratings and surface model: measure a real site before the figure means anything. The last column is everything else alone. Equipment (plug loads) is not controlled, so it never changes.

Comfort: degree-hours outside the occupied band, summed over three zones. Icing hazard: hours with the ground sensor reporting a wet surface at −10..+1 °C while a cable was off. The air thermostat misses ice below −8 °C (Tromsø, Oslo); the sensor mode never does. Almost all the comfort deviation is summer overheating (May to September), not cold rooms: presence lighting with daylight dimming puts less heat into the zones. Winter comfort stays within a few degree-hours in every strategy.
